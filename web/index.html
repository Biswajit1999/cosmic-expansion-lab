THE CEPHEID / RS PUPPIS

A star with
a useful rhythm.

A Cepheid brightens and fades as it pulsates. Its period carries information about its intrinsic luminosity: longer-period classical Cepheids are generally more luminous. Compare that calibrated luminosity with the light we receive, correct for extinction, and a distance becomes possible.

RS Puppis is an especially striking example, surrounded by dust that scatters its changing light. It illustrates what a Cepheid is; it is not presented as one of the individual stars fitted in this project.

What must be calibrated?

The period–luminosity zero point needs an absolute distance anchor. Parallaxes, eclipsing binaries and water masers provide geometric information. Metallicity, dust, photometric calibration and crowding also matter. Our analysis starts downstream of these steps, with published Cepheid-host moduli and their covariance.

THE SUPERNOVA / SN 1994D

A brief flash.
A much longer reach.

A Type Ia supernova is a thermonuclear stellar explosion. Its brightness evolves over weeks, giving observers a light curve rather than a single number. After corrections for light-curve shape, color and other effects, these events become standardizable candles.

That distinction matters: the supernovae do not all have identical uncorrected luminosities. Pantheon+ supplies standardized magnitudes and the covariance needed to propagate statistical and systematic uncertainty. SN 1994D is represented in this release; its nearby redshift excludes it from our default cosmology sample unless flagged as a calibrator.

Why not measure H₀ with supernovae alone?

Brightness gives relative distance very well, but the absolute scale depends on the intrinsic magnitude MB. Increasing H₀ while adjusting MB can leave all predicted apparent magnitudes unchanged. A separate absolute-distance calibration breaks that degeneracy.

RELAXING AN ASSUMPTION

Does one more parameter
earn its place?

A cosmological constant has equation-of-state parameter w = −1. We also fit a flat model with one freely varying, constant w, giving dark energy density proportional to (1 + z)3(1+w).

The extra parameter slightly improves χ², but improvement alone is expected when a model becomes more flexible. The Akaike information criterion adds a parameter penalty. This comparison uses exactly the same observations and covariance.

AIC is a relative model-comparison measure, not a discovery significance. Local Gaussian errors can miss non-Gaussian degeneracies. No evolving-w or CMB/BAO joint fit is claimed.

A SUBTLE BUT ESSENTIAL DISTINCTION

Expansion can accelerate
while H gets smaller.

The scale factor a(t) describes how separations between comoving objects change. The Hubble parameter is the fractional growth rate H = ȧ/a. Acceleration asks whether ȧ itself increases: ä > 0. These are different questions.

In a matter-plus-Λ universe, matter density falls as a⁻³ while the vacuum density remains constant. The expansion eventually accelerates, even while H decreases toward a positive asymptote. In the matter-only comparison, a grows forever but its growth continually decelerates.

Where does acceleration begin in this model?

For flat matter + Λ, q(a) = [Ωₘ/a³ − 2ΩΛ] / [2(Ωₘ/a³ + ΩΛ)]. Acceleration starts at q = 0, or a = [Ωₘ/(2ΩΛ)]1/3. With Ωₘ = 0.315 this corresponds to z ≈ 0.63. It is a model-dependent transition, not a switch at a universal date.

A STANDARD RULER

BAO measures
a fossil sound scale.

Pressure waves in the early photon–baryon plasma left a preferred separation in later matter clustering. Galaxy surveys measure that pattern transversely and along the line of sight, constraining distances and the expansion rate relative to the sound horizon.

A ruler measured in units of its own unknown length does not by itself supply an absolute H₀. An early-universe calculation or another calibration of rd is part of the inference.

A STANDARD SIREN

Listening for
an absolute distance.

The gravitational-wave signal from a compact binary carries distance information. A host redshift completes the connection to expansion. Binary inclination and local peculiar velocities can broaden the result.

The first bright-siren measurement, GW170817 in 2017, reported H₀ = 70+12−8 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ at 68% credibility. This is a historical example with asymmetric uncertainty, not a value fitted or updated by this project.

Questions worth getting right.

What is the universe expanding into?

The standard cosmological model describes changing distances within space. It does not require an external room into which space expands. The observable universe is a region from which signals can have reached us, not necessarily an edge of the whole universe.

Why don’t galaxies, people and atoms expand with it?

The homogeneous expansion model applies on large scales. Bound systems are controlled by local gravitational or electromagnetic interactions. The animation keeps galaxy sizes fixed while their comoving separation changes; it does not model the detailed dynamics of any particular group.

Can a distant galaxy recede faster than light?

In a cosmological coordinate system, recession v = H D can exceed c at sufficiently large proper distance. That is not a nearby object overtaking light in a local inertial frame. It is why a special-relativistic Doppler velocity is not a general interpretation of cosmological redshift.

Does a redder image automatically mean greater redshift?

No. Display colors depend on filters, processing, stellar populations and dust as well as redshift. A scientific redshift comes from identifying spectral features or from calibrated photometric inference, not from judging a picture’s color.

Does dark energy explain what it is?

No. “Dark energy” names the component or effective behavior used to account for accelerated expansion. A cosmological constant is a simple model with w = −1. Fitting it successfully does not establish its microscopic origin.

Why are there 1,701 rows but fewer supernovae?

Some objects have light curves from more than one survey. We keep each row and its correlations. This pinned table contains 1,543 distinct literal CID strings; the publication describes a 1,550-object compilation. Catalog naming and release bookkeeping are not automatically a physical object reconciliation. We report the directly reproducible table count rather than silently replacing it with the headline publication count.

Reproduce the analysis & inspect the assumptions

In the repository root, install dependencies and run:

python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python analysis/download_data.py
python analysis/fit.py
python analysis/research.py
python -m unittest discover -s tests
python -m http.server 8000 --directory web

The pipeline verifies SHA-256 hashes against a pinned upstream commit, fits the full STAT+SYS covariance with a Cholesky solve, and exports the CSV, browser JSON, and publication-ready Hubble diagram. The local fit uses the supplied SH0ES Hubble-flow flags plus calibrators; the wider fit uses zHD > 0.01 plus calibrators. Uncertainties are local Gaussian curvature estimates, not posterior intervals.

Raw spectra, raw Cepheid photometry, BAO measurements and CMB maps are not fitted here. Geometric anchors enter through the released Cepheid-host moduli. This is a reproducible fit to released data products, not a reproduction of every step in the SH0ES pipeline.
